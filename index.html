The year a beach was quietly filled — found by a model no one told what to look for.

You give it a few years of satellite readings for one site. It hands back the date the gravel went down. Here's exactly how, step by step.

01 The recording

Three signals, watched over time

Each satellite pass gives three readings inside the beach: how bright it is, how smooth the surface is, and how wide the beach is. Press play and watch them get recorded, date by date.

steady / normal after the fill
12 dates across three years

02 The fingerprint

All three jump at once — and stay

That's the whole trick. A wet day lifts brightness alone. A storm widens the beach for a week, then it creeps back. Only a real fill moves brightness, texture and width together on the same date and holds them there. Watching three signals instead of one is what tells a fill apart from the weather.

03 The detection

The model names the odd date

For every date, measure how far it breaks from the beach's own steady rhythm. Almost every date barely moves. One doesn't. The model doesn't need you to label anything — it just finds the date that refuses to fit.

how much each date differs from the one before

04 The part everyone asks about

The beach is its own baseline

You never hand the model a threshold or a benchmark. It reads all the dates and works out what "normal" looks like for this beach — the quiet, similar dates. Anything that breaks that pattern stands out on its own. Every beach defines its own normal, so the method travels to any site without retuning.

05 In code it's tiny

No labels, no training set

Unsupervised means there's no answer key. You pass the table; it returns the odd date.

# one row per date, three columns
from sklearn.ensemble import IsolationForest

X   = df[["bright", "texture", "width"]]
iso = IsolationForest(contamination=0.08).fit(X)
df["odd"] = iso.predict(X)        # -1 = doesn't fit

df[df.odd == -1]["date"]         # → the fill date

06 Where to actually start

Just plot it first

Before any model: build that table for one beach and plot the three lines, exactly like step 01. You'll very likely see the jump with your own eyes. The machine learning only turns what you can already see into something you can measure and defend. You don't need it working before you have your first real result.
